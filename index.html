    Typing invoices by hand costs businesses a median of $21.40 per invoice once you count the labor — APQC's 2024–25 Open Standards Benchmarking data, as cited by accounts-payable software vendors. That's before counting the cost of the typos.

    The problem this solves

    Someone on your team is still manually typing numbers off a photo or PDF into a spreadsheet or accounting software — vendor name, line items, totals, tax — one invoice at a time, usually at the end of a long day when mistakes are most likely.

    • It's slow by nature. Manual entry runs at roughly 4–5 invoices an hour once you count the interruptions, so a stack of invoices is a stack of lost hours, not a quick task.
    • Typos get buried until they cause a problem — a wrong total or a double-counted line item usually isn't caught until a payment is already wrong or a vendor calls asking where their money is.

    SnapTally reads the invoice, checks that the numbers actually add up, and puts it in front of a human for a quick yes/no — instead of someone retyping every line by hand and hoping they didn't miss one.
